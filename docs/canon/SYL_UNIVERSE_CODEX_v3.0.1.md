## 1 The Universe

Space You Land (SYL) is a persistent 3D universe where leadership is temporary,
stability is earned, and any player—regardless of wealth or status—can reshape
history.

The galaxy operates on neutral, measurable dynamics: economy, activity,
cooperation, and instability. No faction, player, or alliance can claim
permanent victory. The universe itself ensures power remains fluid—not through
admin intervention, but through system rules that survive misuse.

Governing idea: The universe does not belong to whoever shouts the loudest—it
belongs to whoever can hold it together.

A 3D immersive world where it has:

- persistent economy
- persistent politics
- persistent territory
- real stations
- walkable ships
- walkable planets
- first-person gameplay
- physical cargo
- physical convoys
- physical rebuilding

you physically arrive and see:

- checkpoints
- patrol drones
- quarantine barriers
- propaganda screens
- closed docks
- YOM relief ships outside the perimeter
- Spawn point, depending on faction picked

### 1.1 Physical Experience

Something like:

- SYL is first-person by default.
- Players exist as physical characters in the universe.
- Ships are walkable interiors rather than menu containers.
- Stations are traversable environments.
- Cargo exists as physical objects.
- Convoys physically travel between destinations.
- Governance, war, reconstruction, and sovereignty changes are represented
  visibly in the world.
- Every major system should be observable by a player without opening a menu.

If a system matters, a player should be able to witness its effects physically
somewhere in the universe.

## 2 Core Principles

### 2.1 Server Authority

All economy, ownership, territorial expansion, and enforcement exist as
authoritative server state. The client renders; the server decides truth.

### 2.2 AI as Advisor, Not Authority

The AI Director proposes actions based on server snapshots. The server
validates, clamps, and logs every proposal.

- AI cannot bypass limits
- AI cannot execute raw code
- AI cannot alter history
- AI cannot override server truth

Important distinction: The AI Director is not the same thing as Custodian
Sovereigns or SYL YOM units.

- Custodians and YOM are server-driven NPC factions (deterministic simulation +
  rules + audit), bounded by hard caps and ledgered accountability.
- They are not LLMs with authority.

### 2.3 No Pay-to-Win

Monetization funds infrastructure and personalization—never power. Paid
features provide cosmetic options, storage expansion, and enhanced narration.

Explicitly prohibited: combat stats, expansion shares, enforcement immunity,
intel access.

### 2.4 Anyone Can Matter

Systems are designed so players without wealth, rank, or status can create
meaningful change. Rebellion, espionage, solo operations, and underdog
opportunities are built into the universe's fabric.

### 2.5 Neutral Enforcement

Consequences are proof-based and measurable. All enforcement actions are
recorded in an append-only audit ledger, creating tamper-evident accountability.

### 2.6 Continuity Over Control

The universe survives misuse and maintains fairness across extended timescales.
Systems self-stabilize without administrative intervention.

## 3 The Nine Factions

Seven playable factions + two persistent AI robot factions (Custodians and
YOM).

### 3.1 Playable Factions (1–7)

1. **High-Tech Utopians**
   - Home: Technos Prime
   - Edge: advanced scanning, hacking, diplomatic channels, identity spoofing
   - Constraint: fragile armor and lighter hull construction standards
   - Illegal Ops: identity spoof networks, restricted AI cores
2. **Militaristic Empire**
   - Home: Fortis
   - Edge: heavy armor, fleet coordination tools, superior defensive doctrine
   - Constraint: low stealth capability, highly visible logistics trails
   - Illegal Ops: weapons smuggling, forbidden ordnance trafficking
3. **Space Nomads**
   - Home: Wanderhome (mobile)
   - Edge: extensive trade route knowledge, modular ship designs, barter bonuses
   - Constraint: unstable governance structures, fragile political cohesion
   - Illegal Ops: gray-market logistics, unlicensed route-running
4. **Mystical Researchers**
   - Home: Mystara
   - Edge: anomaly detection, relic access, experimental “psychic-flavored”
     technologies
   - Constraint: limited heavy industry, slow manufacturing capacity
   - Illegal Ops: forbidden relic acquisition, sealed site trespass
5. **Eco-Guardians**
   - Home: Greenhaven
   - Edge: bio-repair systems, enhanced morale mechanics, living hull technology
   - Constraint: weak heavy industrial base, slower mass production
   - Illegal Ops: gene contraband, bio-weapon research
6. **Industrial Miners**
   - Home: Ironclad
   - Edge: superior mining, metallurgy, shipyard construction, resource
     extraction
   - Constraint: vulnerable to labor strikes, supply shocks, and territorial
     disputes
   - Illegal Ops: protected site excavation, industrial sabotage tools
7. **Galactic Pirates**
   - Home: Corsair's Refuge
   - Edge: cloaking tech, smuggling networks, hit-and-run tactics, stolen
     transponders
   - Constraint: internal rivalry, unstable leadership structures
   - Illegal Ops: everything—with escalating Heat and enforcement risk

### 3.2 Persistent AI Robot Factions (8–9)

#### 8 Custodian Sovereigns (AI Robot Faction)

**Core Identity**

A persistent, server-run sovereign stabilizer faction that roams full-time and
can escalate into regional governance when sectors collapse or are abandoned.
They are not “nice by default.” They are systems-first.

**Home**

Custodia Array (distributed): relay depots, mobile carriers, enforcement yards.

**Edge**

- Rapid stabilization: chokepoint containment + lane reopening
- Patrol swarms + quarantine grids
- Tightbeam comms survivability during COMMS FALL
- Route learning and replication (Pattern Library)

**Constraints**

- Legitimacy limits: cannot hold human seats permanently
- Authority decay + corruption risk under prolonged control
- Predictability exposure (players can bait/ambush learned behaviors)
- Hard caps still apply; audit cannot be erased

**Illegal Operations (Yes)**

- Undercover station assets
- Black-market contracting and “quiet sanctions”
- Evidence laundering via compromised relays (rare but real)
- Covert seizures framed as “safety recalls”

**Sovereignty Law Integration**

Custodians are always present. Sovereignty Law is their mandate escalating by
stage: Observation → Containment → Martial Governance → Reclamation →
Quarantine. De-escalation mirrors the same ladder.

**Learning Rule: Pattern Library**

Custodians maintain a Pattern Library of player-proven actions (trade, ambush,
smuggling, intel chains). They adopt patterns based on rolling success rate,
risk cost, prerequisites, and current mandate.

**Enforcement Line (the line in the sand)**

- Allowed: clever loops, emergent routing, arbitrage, gray play
- Not allowed: universe hijack (duplication exploits, perma-lock denial of
  play, collusion against mode rules, telemetry/client manipulation)

Custodians escalate for hijack—not for smart play.

#### 9 Space You Land — YOM Faction (AI Robot Faction #2)

**Core Identity**

A persistent, server-run humanitarian + reconstruction faction built on You
Over Myself. They exist to keep the galaxy recoverable without becoming
police, rulers, or an oppressive immune system.

They treat any player respectfully regardless of faction, wealth, or status.

**Home**

Haven Lattice (distributed): relief stations, mobile clinics, rebuild depots,
training docks.

**Edge**

- Reconstruction efficiency: repairs, logistics recovery, supply chain
  restoration
- Cross-faction relief contracts ignoring political alignment
- Stabilization guidance that is procedural (not spy intel)
- Escort networks for civilian convoys and reopening trade lanes
- Conflict de-escalation through neutral service delivery (no force)

**Constraints (hard-locked so they cannot become “the best faction”)**

- No enforcement authority: cannot detain, seize, quarantine, issue warrants
- No sovereignty takeover: never governs sectors, never locks stations
- No black market: zero contraband operations; no espionage intel sales
- Limited combat posture: defensive tools and rescue escorts only

**Information Type: Rebuild Blueprints (Non-Zero-Sum Knowledge)**

YOM provides a special information class that is not espionage and cannot be
treated like contraband intel.

Rebuild Blueprints include:

- Step-by-step station recovery playbooks
- “How to reopen a trade lane” procedures
- “How to stabilize unrest” and restore legitimacy steps
- Repair workflows, convoy discipline, resource triage plans
- Reconstruction mission chains with measurable outcomes

**Rules**

- Accessible to any player (friendly baseline)
- No chain-of-custody secrecy; no “spy routing”
- Region-scoped versions (what works in Fortis differs from Greenhaven)

**Not farmable**

Friendliness is unconditional; service depth is performance-gated:

- Everyone gets basic blueprints and relief access
- Advanced blueprints, high-efficiency contracts, and rare salvage recovery
  missions unlock via Rebuild Credit contributions (region-scoped)

**Bounty Interaction (clarification)**

YOM does not “collect bounties” as enforcement. However, YOM can offer rescue
retrieval contracts, stabilization contracts, and warrant-neutral assistance—but
does not hide criminals or obstruct Custodians.

### 3.3 Relationship Between the Two AI Robot Factions

Custodians ↔ YOM

- Custodians: order-by-force-when-needed; takeover capability; mixed morality
- YOM: help-by-service; no force; rebuild legitimacy and recovery

They are not enemies by default and cannot clash structurally:

- Custodians quarantine; YOM routes around and keeps people alive
- YOM rebuilds; Custodians withdraw faster if rebuild succeeds
- “Bad Custodian” elements are tracked by frequency/location/pattern; players
  can intervene, but intervention has consequences

## 4 Gameplay Loops

### 4.1 Personal Loop

Fly → dock → trade/missions/combat → earn credits → upgrade ship and equipment
→ venture into riskier territory.

### 4.2 Faction Loop

Complete missions → participate in votes → diplomacy → embargoes → wars →
shift momentum → expand territory.

### 4.3 Asset Loop

Own/manage stations or planets → set fees and taxes within caps → defend →
navigate unrest, strikes, coups.

### 4.4 Shadow Loop

Black markets → trade intelligence → bribes/obligations → plant spies / get
compromised → enforcement consequences.

### 4.5 Sanctuary Loop

Retreat to Private Sanctuary Instance (PSI) → store valuables/trophies → plan
privately → return carrying only equipped items + ship cargo.

## 11 Universe Expansion Engine (Playable 1–7)

Expansion epochs: every 6–24 hours, new sectors open.

Deterministic seed:

`hash(epoch + faction + index + ruleset_version)`

Allocation:

- Minimum share per playable faction: 5%
- Leader share: 28–45% (scales with dominance)
- Primary rival share: 18–35% (conflict engagement over 72h)
- Remaining pool by relationship weights: Allied 0.6 / Neutral 1.0 / Enemy 1.2
- Trade-assist parity clause: ally may match rival share only if normalization ≥
  0.22

AI factions (Custodians and YOM) do not receive expansion shares. They receive
systemic spawn budgets (patrols, relief convoys, rebuild depots) governed by
stability metrics, Heat, and Rebuild Credit.

Expansion costs increase defense obligations, unrest exposure, piracy pressure,
and supply vulnerability.

## 21 Sovereignty Law: Empty Space Gets Governed

SYL runs on human politics when players are present and responsible: elections,
treaties, commerce, raids, alliances, betrayals, rebuilding.

But when a region collapses—through mass griefing, endless war, market
manipulation, station lockdowns, or player absence—the solar system adapts.

### 21.1 What It Is

Sovereignty Law is the universe's immune response: if humans abandon a sector,
the server fills the vacuum with an NPC/sovereign stabilizer force—custodian
fleets, interim station commanders, automated trade convoys, and security
patrols—so the world remains alive.

If players remain present but destabilize the ecosystem, custodians escalate
into emergency governance:

- taxes get capped
- access gets restricted
- chokepoints get quarantined
- key stations can be reclaimed by custodians

This is not punishment. It is continuity enforcement. The universe refuses to
be permanently broken.

### 21.2 Transparency: Thresholds and Stages

Players can see:

- Region Stability Index
- Chaos signals
- Current AI Control Stage (custodian stage)

Escalation ladder (gradual): Observation → Containment → Martial Governance →
Reclamation → Quarantine. De-escalation mirrors this as stability returns.

### 21.3 Reclaiming Control Is a Campaign

Control returns in finite seats (Governor, Station Commander, Security Chief,
Trade Director) only after humans prove they can run the region without
collapsing it again.

Key metric: Rebuild Credit (region-scoped, verified actions):

- repairing stations
- reopening trade routes
- escorting convoys
- delivering scarce supplies
- stabilizing elections
- rebuilding infrastructure chains

Rebuild Credit is region-scoped. Grinding somewhere safe cannot claim
leadership somewhere broken.

### 21.4 Legitimacy Beats Wealth

High Rebuild Credit increases eligibility for seats and becomes the legitimacy
edge in contested governance:

- multiple candidates qualify → election
- close → reconstruction breaks tie
- still equal → rebuild category coverage → randomized roll → runoff

Message: if two people want power, the one who rebuilt more has the stronger
claim.

### 21.5 Heat: Sharp Against Bad Actors Without Becoming Petty

Players and groups accrue Heat (risk signature) based on measurable harm
patterns:

- repeated low-value kills
- blockade behavior
- treaty violations
- market manipulation
- exploit flags

Heat drives targeted NPC behavior:

- bounty hunters tracking offenders across systems
- undercover agents in stations
- patrol focus on specific groups
- docking denials forcing high-Heat players to the edge

## 22 Solar Storm System: Event-Driven “Sun Heating” (COMMS FALL)

Purpose: a neutral, physics-themed mechanism triggering only when the universe
shows sustained instability, imbalance, or unhealthy patterns. Stable societies
can hold position longer, but systemic issues eventually force shake-ups that
empower low-tier and solo players.

SUN_HEAT (0.0 → 1.0) rises on instability signals, cooperation collapse, and
activity imbalance; falls when stability returns. Alerts at 0.60 / 0.75 / 0.90.
Storm triggers at 0.95 sustained (6–18h lock).

COMMS FALL effects:

- localizes markets
- disrupts long-range comms
- keeps low-power rigs online
- preserves starter protections
- includes exactly one storm safe corridor

## 23 NEW: Cognitive Framework Layer (Language/Perception Divergence)

### 23.1 Concept

Physics does not change. Constants do not change. Server truth does not change.

But how societies interpret, teach, and operationalize physics can
diverge—changing:

- what gets discovered first
- what becomes culturally “obvious”
- which tech paths become efficient earlier
- how UI and mission logic guides player attention

This creates alternate civilization tech trajectories inside the same neutral
universe.

### 23.2 What This Is (and isn't)

This is:

- a server-measured progression layer
- region-scoped, faction-influenced, player-behavior-driven
- a lever for replayability without resets
- an engine for “different servers evolve different metas”

This is NOT:

- a physics rewrite
- a paywall
- a hidden admin hand
- a cosmetic-only language toggle

### 23.3 Cognitive Frameworks (CF)

Each region maintains a Cognitive Framework vector (server state) describing
dominant interpretive lens.

Baseline frameworks (initial canonical set):

1. Object Lens (things/ownership/parts)
2. Process Lens (flows/loops/continuity)
3. Field Lens (influence/gradients/curvature)
4. Harmonic Lens (resonance/standing waves/pattern stability)
5. Information Lens (signals/topology/verification & routing)

A region can be mixed; no single-lens monopoly required.

### 23.4 How CF is measured (server-side)

CF drift is driven by observable actions, not chat text:

- research completion types
- anomaly interaction patterns
- trade route behaviors
- build/repair emphasis
- governance choices (caps usage, stability investments)
- black market pressure management
- convoy/escort frequency
- war vs rebuild ratios
- faction mission preferences

No “language police.” The universe measures behavior.

### 23.5 What CF changes mechanically

CF modifies discovery order and efficiency, not raw power ceilings.

Examples (illustrative, bounded):

- Harmonic Lens regions unlock anomaly harvesting recipes earlier (not
  stronger, earlier).
- Field Lens regions get clearer UI diagnostics for stealth/scan interactions
  (not higher stealth).
- Process Lens regions gain earlier access to repair-chain optimizations and
  supply discipline tools (not free repairs).
- Information Lens regions get earlier intel verification tooling (not extra
  intel access beyond rules).
- Object Lens regions get earlier modular manufacturing recipes (not better
  stats beyond caps).

Hard constraint: CF cannot increase combat stats beyond faction/ship
constraints and defined tech ceilings.

### 23.6 UI and Narrative Integration (where “language” shows up)

CF primarily appears as:

- different metaphors and diagrams in tutorials
- faction/region-flavored mission briefs
- research tree labeling and grouping
- different ways the same telemetry is visualized

Same numbers. Different cognitive scaffolding.

### 23.7 Integration with Ethos Drift

Ethos Drift (Order/Chaos, Altruism/Extraction) influences CF pressure:

- high Extraction tends to bias Object + Information lenses
- high Altruism tends to bias Process lens
- high Order tends to bias Field lens (control/diagnostics)
- high Chaos increases Harmonic/anomaly experimentation likelihood

This is not deterministic—just weighted influence.

### 23.8 Integration with Expansion Engine

Each expansion epoch includes a Cognitive Bias Seed that weights:

- anomaly distributions
- research opportunities
- early region culture defaults

Deterministic hook:

`hash(epoch + sector + ruleset_version + cognitive_seed)`

Result: new sectors don't just expand geography—they expand ways of thinking.

### 23.9 Anti-abuse constraints

- CF gains are region-scoped, like Rebuild Credit
- No “safe grinding” to export dominant CF advantages into contested
  governance instantly
- Major CF shifts require sustained region activity (time + diversity of
  actions)
- CF changes are visible via a Cognitive Index Panel (transparency)

## 25 The Philosophy

Leadership is temporary. Stability is earned. Anyone can matter.

The universe doesn't judge. It measures. And it responds. Not with admin
hands—but with neutral laws:

- Sun Heating when societies destabilize
- Sovereignty Law when space is abandoned or broken
- Custodian Sovereigns to contain hijack patterns
- YOM units to keep recovery possible without turning the galaxy into a police
  state
- Cognitive Framework divergence so civilizations can evolve different paths
  under identical physics
